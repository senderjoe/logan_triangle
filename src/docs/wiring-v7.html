Tags: every 5V joins together, every GND joins together, and both drivers' ENA go to Q1's C. The Micro has one +5V pin (left-hand header), so split it 5 ways (2 × OPTO, 2 × encoder RED, R1) with a terminal strip or lever connectors. The encoder BLK wires and Q1's E go to the GND pin on the right-hand header (either GND works). The PSU −V does not connect to the Micro.
⚠ Never plug or unplug the green terminal blocks while powered.
Keep PUL/DIR and encoder wires ≥ 10 cm from the motor leads. The motor and encoder cables both use RED/BLK/YEL/BLU, so label them.

AccelStepper stepperLeft (DRIVER, 15, 14); // PUL, DIR

Encoder encoderLeft (0, 1);   // index Z: D2

DRIVER_ENABLE = A0;  // LOW = drivers on, via Q1
// START button A5 (not wired yet)

Notes

  • No resistors on PUL/DIR. The DM320T inputs are opto-isolated, wired common-anode: OPTO to +5 V, and the Micro pulls PUL and DIR low (manual fig. 2).
  • Q1 holds the drivers off (motors free, no jerk) whenever A0 isn't driven: at power-on, uploads and resets. The program pulls A0 LOW on r. Check: before r, the shafts turn by hand.
  • Supply. A separate pair of wires from the PSU to each driver; 12 V is quieter at slow speeds. The PSU ground stays separate from the Micro (the opto inputs isolate them). Fit the mains cover.
  • Encoder supply 3.3–5.5 V, so the Micro's 5 V is fine. The outputs are 5 V logic, straight to the Micro.
  • Unused encoder wires (ORG, GRN, WHT) are driven outputs: insulate each one separately. Don't ground them.
  • Driver red LED: 1 flash = over-current, 2 = over-voltage (> 34 V). Power off and on to reset.
  • ⚠ Same pins as the carrier board (board.pdf), so this tests the board's pin map before it's built. Recheck directions with the test sketch after rewiring.
